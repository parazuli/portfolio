## Overall Impression

The site's ceiling is genuinely high — the Nepal Airlines case study is the kind of self-directed audit rigor that would impress both a client and a recruiter, and the accessibility instincts baked into the CSS (a `prefers-reduced-motion` block disabling nearly every animation, consistent `:focus-visible` outlines) show more system-level thinking than most competitor portfolios. But the site currently fails its own standard: the "What I do" tabs don't respond to click or keyboard at all, and 3 of 4 project cards are dead clicks sitting one section below the site's best moment. The biggest opportunity isn't more visual polish — it's making the rest of the site behave with the same rigor the Nepal Airlines write-up describes.

## What's Working

1. **Nepal Airlines case study structure** — naming three considered-and-rejected directions plus a documented iteration table is exactly the "product-thinking rigor" your own positioning calls for, and it's rare in portfolio sites.
2. **Accessibility intent in the CSS layer** — a single `prefers-reduced-motion` block disables essentially every animation including the tools marquee, and `:focus-visible` is defined consistently across nav, buttons, and social links.
3. **Typographic restraint** — the italic display-name treatment and a consistent `--radius`/`--shadow` token scale read as an authored system, not a component-library default.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] "What I do" skills tabs have no click or keyboard handler — mobile visitors may see this section permanently stuck**
- Why it matters: `onMouseEnter={() => setOpenService(s.id)}` (App.tsx:437) is the only trigger — no `onClick`, no `onKeyDown`. Keyboard users can Tab to the button but Enter/Space does nothing (WCAG 2.1.1 failure). On touch, a single tap typically doesn't reliably fire synthetic `mouseenter` — meaning phone visitors may be stuck on the default "Web Design" panel for the entire visit.
- Fix: add `onClick={() => setOpenService(s.id)}` alongside the hover enhancement, and implement the standard WAI-ARIA tabs keyboard pattern (arrow keys move focus + selection).
- Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] Work-grid cards share identical hover treatment but only 1 of 4 actually does anything**
- Why it matters: All four `.work-card` buttons get the same `scale(1.01)` hover + image zoom regardless of whether `onClick` exists (IdeaX/Livora/Nepal Tourism have `onClick: undefined`, App.tsx:376-393). Creates the site's one real emotional valley right after its strongest asset.
- Fix: differentiate non-linked cards visually (muted/no-zoom hover) and give them an honest state — "Case study coming soon," or link out to the real deliverable if one exists.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Three of four project thumbnails are generic stock "abstract art," not real work**
- Why it matters: For a UI/UX portfolio, decorative Unsplash gradients standing in for product shots is the biggest specificity failure found — it's the first thing both a hiring manager and a client scan, and it currently signals "template." Also brushes against PRODUCT.md's provenance-honesty constraint.
- Fix: replace with real screenshots/mockups from the actual hackathon or self-learning projects, even rough ones.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable audit` or `$impeccable document`

**[P2] "How I work" process copy is generic and contradicts stated AI-fluency positioning**
- Why it matters: The four steps are the stock "Discover / Define / Design / Deliver" template (App.tsx:498-501); the Design step credits only Figma. PRODUCT.md says AI-tool fluency must be treated as a stated professional capability — yet the section titled "How I work" omits it entirely.
- Fix: rewrite each step's one-liner to name a concrete tool/output tied to the actual process.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

**[P3] Orphaned animated stat counter, dead CSS, and generic type/background choices**
- Why it matters: A fully-built project-count animation (App.tsx:20-21, 104-128) never renders. `src/index.css` carries ~150+ lines of orphaned selectors. Detector's real hits — Inter font, gradient-text, grid background — read as generic-modern rather than authored specifically.
- Fix: wire the counter to a real stat element or delete the effect; prune dead CSS; consider a less-default type pairing and toning down/removing the grid background.
- Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`
