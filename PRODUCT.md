# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Two audiences, weighted evenly: potential freelance/agency clients evaluating whether to hire Rohan for a project, and recruiters/hiring managers evaluating him for a full-time or contract UI/UX role. Both should come away convinced of hire-worthy craft and process, and act via the contact section (email, or social links).

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Rohan Parajuli, a UI/UX and graphic designer based in Kathmandu, Nepal, working across web design, product design, brand identity, and graphic design. It exists to showcase his work and process and convert visits into freelance inquiries or job consideration.

## Positioning

Two things held equally, not one over the other:

- **Product-thinking rigor**: goes beyond visuals to audit real systems, write problem statements, and iterate like a product designer (demonstrated in the Nepal Airlines case study — a self-directed booking-flow audit with a stated problem statement, rejected/chosen directions, and a documented iteration history).
- **AI-augmented workflow**: works fluently with Claude, ChatGPT, and Stitch AI as active tools in the design process, alongside Figma/Photoshop/Illustrator/InDesign — named explicitly as tool badges, not hidden.

## Operating Context

Single-page site with sections for About, Featured Projects, What I Do (services), Tools, Process, and Contact. One featured project (Nepal Airlines) expands into an in-page case-study overlay with a full write-up (problem, problem statement, approach, design iterations table, reflection); the others are gallery cards without an expanded case study yet.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Featured project provenance is mixed and should stay honestly distinguishable rather than presented as uniform client work: some are real work from hackathons, some are self-learning/practice projects, and some are self-directed case studies (e.g. Nepal Airlines, explicitly framed in its own copy as a self-directed audit, not a commissioned engagement). Future case-study copy must preserve this honesty rather than implying paid client work where none exists.
- Contact is via email (rohanparajuli78@gmail.com) and social links (Instagram, LinkedIn, Dribbble) — no contact form.
- No CMS/backend — content is hardcoded in `src/App.tsx`; edits happen there.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Rohan Parajuli. Logo mark + wordmark in the header.
- Location: Kathmandu, Nepal — stated in the hero and footer.
- Tool badges (Figma, Photoshop, Illustrator, Claude, ChatGPT, InDesign, Stitch AI) are a deliberate, named part of his identity as a designer who works with AI tools, not incidental.
- Voice: direct, lowercase-casual confidence (e.g. "Booking a flight shouldn't feel like three apps"), not corporate-formal.

## Evidence on Hand

- Nepal Airlines case study: full self-directed audit write-up already exists in `src/App.tsx` (problem, problem statement, approach, iteration table, reflection). Real content, not placeholder.
- IdeaX 2026, Livora, and Nepal Tourism are gallery-only project cards today with short descriptions but no expanded case study; their descriptions should not overstate client engagement given the mixed hackathon/self-learning/case-study provenance above.
- No testimonials, press, or client logos exist; none should be fabricated.

## Product Principles

1. Show real process, not just polished final shots — the Nepal Airlines case study is the model to extend to, not deviate from.
2. Never blur self-directed/practice work into implied paid client work; let honest provenance coexist with strong craft.
3. Serve freelance-client and recruiter audiences with the same content — no separate tracks, no favoring one over the other.
4. Treat AI-tool fluency (Claude, ChatGPT, Stitch AI) as a stated professional capability, on par with traditional design tools, not a novelty aside.
